White rings are the direct sound from the soundboard, brighter toward the open lid. Blue rings are the first reflections from the walls. The bars show the relative level reaching each microphone before any gain is added. The piece is the opening of Bach's Prelude in C major (BWV 846), synthesised in the browser.

The shaded pickup fields show where each microphone hears best. They are not hard edges: sound from outside a field is still recorded, only quieter. Before you press record

  1. Turn on 48 V phantom power for inputs 3 and 4 on H6 #1, and use external power if you can.
  2. Put the lid on full stick, then find the sweet spot on headphones while someone plays.
  3. Check levels on both H6s. Have the pianist play the loudest passage of the piece at full performance strength. Set each gain knob (X/Y capsule, inputs 3 and 4 on H6 #1, X/Y on H6 #2) so the peaks hit about −12 dB and never pass −6 dB. Players often play louder in the concert than in the soundcheck, so this leaves room. If the clip light comes on, turn the gain down and test again.
  4. Start both H6s, then clap once in front of the piano. Clap again at the end so the tracks can be synced in editing.
  5. Blend levels and timing later in the mix.
